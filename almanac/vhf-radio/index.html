VHF radio

The channels at sea and inland, your waters first.

The channels a boater uses most, at sea and inland. Transmitting needs an operator certificate. Locks, bridges, ports and marinas have their own channels: those are on the signs, the chart and in the local almanac.

At sea, everywhere

The international channels, the same on every marine radio.

  • 16
    Distress, safety and calling. Keep a listening watch on it at sea.
  • 70
    DSC alerts and calls only. Never voice.
  • 6
    Ship to ship, and search and rescue with ships and aircraft.
  • 13
    Bridge to bridge: navigation safety between ships.
  • 72 · 77
    Ship to ship.
  • 15 · 17
    Talk on board, at 1 W at most.
  • AIS 1 · 2
    AIS data only, no voice.

Simplex channels use one frequency, so one station talks at a time. Duplex channels pair a ship and a shore frequency and work only with a coast station.

Port, pilot and traffic channels are on the chart and in the almanac for the area.

Source: ITU Radio Regulations, Appendix 18.

Inland waterways in Europe

Rivers, canals and lakes in the countries of the Basel arrangement (RAINWAT) follow their own radio rules.

  • 10
    Ship to ship, unless the authority sets another channel.
  • 16
    Distress, safety and calling only where the waterway meets the sea. Not allowed in Austria, Luxembourg and Switzerland.
  • 70
    No DSC on inland waterways.
  • 15 · 17
    Talk on board only.

Every radio sends your boat's ATIS code when you let go of the talk button; ATIS is required inland.

Ship to ship, port and on-board channels run at 0.5 to 1 W, switched down by the radio.

No dual watch inland. Keep to safety and navigation, except on the channels set aside for other talk.

Nautical information, lock and bridge channels are set per stretch: on the signs and in the waterway guide.

Source: RAINWAT, Regional Arrangement on the Radiocommunication Service for Inland Waterways, 2012, amended 2023.

Dutch waters

The inland waterway rules for Europe apply, plus the BPR.

  • 10
    Ship to ship, unless a blokkanaal is set for the stretch.
  • Blokkanaal
    The traffic post's channel: on the signs and the Rijkswaterstaat marifoon map.
  • 67 · 73
    The Coastguard's on-scene search and rescue on the Waddenzee, IJsselmeer, Schelde and Eems-Dollard.
  • 70
    DSC allowed on the Waddenzee, IJsselmeer, Eems-Dollard and Ooster- and Westerschelde, on a voluntary basis.

With a marifoon aboard, have it on and listen while you are underway (BPR article 4.05).

Marinas, bridges and locks have their own channel: on the signs and in the almanac.

Source: BPR article 4.05; RAINWAT Annex 2; Rijkswaterstaat.

German waters

The inland waterway rules for Europe apply, plus the BinSchStrO. At sea, the international channels.

  • 10
    Ship to ship. With a radio aboard, report before blind stretches, narrow fairways and bridge openings.
  • Sign B.11
    Radio compulsory on the stretch, on the channel the sign shows.
  • 16 · 70
    At sea: Bremen Rescue Radio (DGzRS) keeps watch, 24 hours.

Inland radio needs the UBI certificate.

Nautical information channels (Revierzentralen) are on the signs and in ELWIS.

Source: BinSchStrO § 4.05; DGzRS; RAINWAT.

French waters

Inland, the inland waterway rules for Europe. At sea, the CROSS.

  • 10
    Inland: the first ship to ship channel, at 1 W.
  • 16
    At sea: call the CROSS directly in distress. Special weather bulletins go out on it too.
  • 70
    At sea only: DSC.

At sea in French waters the permis plaisance covers the use of a VHF. Inland, a handheld up to 6 W needs no certificate, except on the Moselle from Metz to the border.

Each lock and VNF sector has its own channel: on the sign at the lock and in the waterway guide.

Source: Ministère de la Mer; ANFR, Manuel CRR fluvial; RAINWAT.

UK waters

The international channels, plus these.

  • 16
    Distress and urgency calls; keep a listening watch. The Coastguard announces safety information here.
  • 62 · 63 · 64
    The Coastguard reads safety information and weather after the call on 16; sometimes on 10.
  • 80
    Marinas and yacht clubs.
  • M · M2
    Marinas and yacht clubs, in UK waters only.

Harbour and port operations channels are on the chart and in the almanac.

Source: Maritime and Coastguard Agency; Ofcom ship radio licence guidance.

US waters

Set the radio to the US channel plan. Some radios show 22A as 1022.

  • 16
    Distress, safety and calling. The Coast Guard keeps watch.
  • 9
    Boater calling.
  • 22A
    Coast Guard liaison and safety broadcasts, announced on 16.
  • 13
    Bridge to bridge, at 1 W. Ships of 20 m and more listen.
  • 68 · 69 · 71 · 78A
    Recreational working channels.
  • 72
    Recreational, ship to ship only.
  • WX1–WX7
    NOAA weather.

VTS, port and bridge channels are on the chart and in the Coast Pilot.

Source: US Coast Guard Navigation Center; 47 CFR 80.373.

Canadian waters

The Coast Guard (MCTS) listens on 16 and 70, not on working channels.

  • 16
    Distress, urgency, safety and calling. Call the Coast Guard here first.
  • 70
    DSC only, no voice.
  • 68
    The general channel for recreational boaters.
  • 69 · 72
    Pleasure craft on the BC coast.
  • 71
    Marinas and yacht clubs, East Coast and Lake Winnipeg.
  • 21B · 83B · WX
    Weather and marine broadcasts, receive only.

78A is not a pleasure craft channel in Canada.

In port, turn the power down to 1 W. Transmitting needs the ROC-M.

Working channels differ by region; MCTS and VTS channels are in Radio Aids to Marine Navigation.

Source: Canadian Coast Guard, Radio Aids to Marine Navigation; ISED RBR-2; Transport Canada TP 511.

New Zealand waters

There is no shore DSC in New Zealand: make a distress call by voice on 16.

  • 16
    Distress and calling. Maritime Radio keeps watch; weather and warnings are announced here.
  • 67 · 68 · 69 · 71
    Maritime Radio working channels, by station.
  • 6 · 8
    Ship to ship.
  • 73
    Marinas.
  • 1–5 · 7 · 18 · 60–66
    Repeaters for Coastguard and coastal stations, by area.
  • 19 · 20 · 79
    Coastguard local weather, broadcast all the time.

Transmitting needs the Maritime VHF Operator's Certificate and a call sign.

Which Maritime Radio and Coastguard channel covers your area is in the Maritime NZ Radio Handbook.

Source: Maritime NZ, Radio Handbook (April 2025).
