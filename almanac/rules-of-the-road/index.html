Rules of the road

Sound signals, lights and day shapes, from the rules at sea.

The signals of the COLREGs, the rules at sea, in short. Not the official text.

Sound signals in sight

  • I am altering my course to starboard.
  • I am altering my course to port.
  • I am operating astern propulsion.
  • I don't understand your intentions, or doubt you are doing enough to avoid a collision.
  • Coming up to a bend or a spot where boats may be hidden. A boat behind it answers the same.

Overtaking in a narrow channel

  • I intend to overtake you on your starboard side.
  • I intend to overtake you on your port side.
  • Agreed: the boat ahead lets the other pass.

In fog, every 2 minutes

  • Power-driven, making way through the water.
  • Power-driven, under way but stopped.
  • Sailing, fishing, towing, not under command or restricted in maneuvering.
  • Being towed, sounded right after the tow's signal.
  • At anchor, to warn a boat coming close. Besides the bell.

At anchor: the bell rung rapidly for 5 seconds every minute.

Aground: three strokes of the bell, the rapid ringing, three strokes again.

Under 12 m a boat may give any other clear sound signal instead, at least every two minutes.

A short blast is about 1 second, a prolonged one 4 to 6.

Lights, seen from ahead

Her starboard green is on your left, port red on your right. Both: she's coming straight at you.

  • Power-drivenA white masthead light, red and green sidelights and a white sternlight. Over 50 m a second masthead light, further aft and higher.
  • Under sailSidelights and a sternlight, no masthead light. Under 20 m they can be one tricolor at the top of the mast. Engine on: power-driven lights.
  • Small motorboatUnder 7 m and no faster than 7 knots: one all-round white light is enough.
  • At anchorOne all-round white light forward. Over 50 m a second, lower one aft.
  • Not under commandTwo all-round reds, one above the other: she can't keep out of your way. Making way: sidelights and sternlight too.
  • Restricted in ability to maneuverRed, white, red all-round, one above the other: working, such as dredging or laying cable. Making way: her usual lights too.
  • Constrained by her draftThree all-round reds over her power-driven lights: a deep ship bound to the channel. Keep clear.
  • TrawlingGreen over white all-round. Making way: sidelights and sternlight too.
  • Fishing, not trawlingRed over white all-round. Making way: sidelights and sternlight too.
  • Pilot on dutyWhite over red all-round. Under way: sidelights and sternlight too.
  • TowingTwo masthead lights one above the other, three when the tow is over 200 m. From astern, a yellow towing light above the sternlight.
  • AgroundHer anchor light, with two all-round reds one above the other.

Day shapes

  • At anchor.
  • Under sail with the engine on: power-driven.
  • Not under command.
  • Restricted in ability to maneuver.
  • Constrained by her draft.
  • Fishing or trawling.
  • Towing, or being towed, with a tow over 200 m.
  • Aground.

Inland waters have their own rules: CEVNI on most European waterways, the BPR in the Netherlands, the RPR on the Rhine. Their lights and signals differ in places. The official rulebooks.
